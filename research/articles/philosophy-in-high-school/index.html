Philosophy in High School: The Importance of Incorporating Critical Thinking and Ethics in Social Studies, English, and Science Curricula

Benjamin A. Moss · Originally published on LinkedIn

View original on LinkedIn

"Philosophy in High School: The Importance of Incorporating Critical Thinking and Ethics in Social Studies, English, and Science Curricula”

Abstract: 

This paper argues that high school curriculum in social studies, English, and science should integrate philosophical topics that emphasize critical thinking and ethics. The benefits of incorporating philosophy include the development of critical thinking skills, cultivation of ethical thinking, and fostering intellectual engagement. However, challenges such as time constraints, teacher preparation, and student engagement may arise. Despite these challenges, the potential benefits of philosophical inquiry make it a worthwhile endeavor. Teachers and school administrators should consider the benefits of integrating philosophical topics into the high school curriculum.


I. Introduction

Explanation of the purpose of the paper

The purpose of this paper is to argue for the inclusion of philosophical topics in high school curricula, specifically in social studies, English, and science. As noted by the American Philosophical Association (2016), incorporating philosophy into secondary education has been shown to improve critical thinking, problem-solving, and communication skills, as well as promote ethical and civic awareness. Additionally, incorporating philosophical discussions into these subjects can help students engage with complex ideas and develop a deeper understanding of the material (Lipman, 2003).

The paper will explore the benefits that incorporating philosophical discussions into these subjects can provide. For instance, integrating philosophical topics into social studies can help students analyze different perspectives on social issues such as justice, freedom, and equality. Similarly, in science, students can evaluate the evidence for different scientific theories and consider the implications of scientific discoveries from an ethical standpoint. In English classes, students can analyze literary works that raise ethical questions and explore different moral perspectives.

The paper will also address potential challenges to integrating philosophical topics, including time constraints, teacher preparation, and student engagement. While these challenges exist, there are ways to overcome them, such as effective time management strategies, professional development opportunities for teachers, and using relevant examples to engage students.

Ultimately, the goal of this paper is to encourage schools to consider the benefits of philosophical inquiry and strive to incorporate philosophical topics into their high school curricula, in order to help students become more well-rounded and informed citizens who are equipped with critical thinking skills, ethical reasoning, and a love of learning.


Importance of incorporating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy

Incorporating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy is crucial for several reasons. According to Lipman (2003), philosophy provides a framework for students to think critically and deeply about complex issues. By engaging with philosophical concepts, students can learn to evaluate evidence, analyze arguments, and develop their own ideas. This is particularly important in an age where misinformation and fake news are prevalent, as students need to be equipped with the skills to navigate complex and contradictory information.

Moreover, incorporating philosophical discussions in high school curricula can help students cultivate ethical thinking skills. According to the American Philosophical Association (2016), philosophy provides a foundation for moral reasoning and ethical decision-making. By engaging with philosophical topics, students can learn to consider different perspectives, examine their own beliefs and biases, and develop a more nuanced understanding of complex ethical issues.

Finally, incorporating philosophical discussions into high school curricula can foster intellectual engagement and curiosity. As noted by Nussbaum (2010), philosophy is an inherently interdisciplinary field that can help students see the connections between different subjects. By engaging with philosophical concepts in social studies, English, and science classes, students can develop a deeper understanding of the material and a love of learning that extends beyond the classroom.

Overall, incorporating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy is important for developing critical thinking skills, ethical reasoning, and intellectual engagement. By incorporating philosophy into social studies, English, and science curricula, schools can help students become more well-rounded and informed citizens.

Incorporating philosophical topics into high school curricula provides several benefits for students, including the development of critical thinking skills, cultivation of ethical thinking, and fostering of intellectual engagement (American Philosophical Association, 2016; Lipman, 2003; Nussbaum, 2010). However, there are also challenges associated with incorporating philosophical discussions in the high school classroom.

One of the main challenges is time constraints. Teachers may struggle to find the time to cover all the required material and also engage students in philosophical discussions. As noted by Laverty and Rincke (2018), incorporating philosophical discussions into the curriculum requires careful planning and time management to ensure that all necessary material is covered while also providing students with meaningful philosophical inquiry.

Another challenge is teacher preparation. Incorporating philosophical topics into the high school curriculum requires teachers to have a strong background in philosophy. However, not all teachers may feel comfortable leading philosophical discussions, or they may lack the necessary training to do so effectively. As noted by Cohen and Schatz (2017), professional development programs can help teachers develop the necessary skills to incorporate philosophical discussions into their classrooms.

A third challenge is student engagement. Some students may not be interested in philosophical discussions or may struggle to understand complex philosophical concepts. As noted by Irvin and Bizzell (2018), it is important to engage students with relevant examples and to make philosophical topics accessible to all students, regardless of their prior knowledge or interest in philosophy.

Overall, while there are challenges associated with incorporating philosophical discussions into the high school curriculum, the potential benefits make it a worthwhile endeavor. With careful planning, teacher preparation, and attention to student engagement, schools can successfully incorporate philosophical topics into social studies, English, and science curricula to help students develop critical thinking skills, cultivate ethical thinking, and foster intellectual engagement.


II. Benefits of Integrating Philosophical Topics

Integrating philosophical topics into high school curricula has numerous benefits for students. Three major benefits are the development of critical thinking skills, the cultivation of ethical thinking, and the fostering of intellectual engagement.

One benefit of incorporating philosophy into high school curricula is the development of critical thinking skills. Philosophy involves the analysis of complex arguments and the evaluation of evidence, which can help students develop their reasoning and analytical skills (Schratz & Walker, 2017). For example, when studying social studies, students can analyze different perspectives on social issues such as justice, freedom, and equality, which requires them to consider multiple points of view and evaluate evidence to form their own opinions (Perry, 2018).

Another benefit of integrating philosophical topics is the cultivation of ethical thinking. Philosophy provides a framework for ethical thinking and moral reasoning (Mikhail, 2018). By studying ethical theories and examining ethical dilemmas in literature, science, and social studies, students can develop their own ethical reasoning skills (Pizarro & Bloom, 2003). This can help students become more thoughtful and conscientious citizens.

Finally, integrating philosophical discussions into high school curricula can foster intellectual engagement and curiosity. Philosophy encourages students to question assumptions, explore diverse perspectives, and engage in critical reflection (Lipman, Sharp, & Oscanyan, 1980). By incorporating philosophical discussions into classes such as science and social studies, students may become more interested in these subjects and develop a love of learning (Schratz & Walker, 2017).


Develop critical thinking skills

Integrating philosophical topics in high school curricula can help develop critical thinking skills. This is because philosophy involves the analysis of complex arguments and the evaluation of evidence, which can be applied to various fields of study. According to a study by the American Philosophical Association, philosophy education can improve critical thinking skills, which are essential for academic and professional success (Fischer, 2015).

In social studies classes, for instance, students can analyze different perspectives on social issues such as justice, freedom, and equality, which requires them to evaluate evidence and make informed judgments. In science classes, students can evaluate the evidence for different scientific theories and consider the implications of scientific discoveries. By engaging in philosophical inquiry, students can learn to question assumptions, identify biases, and develop a deeper understanding of complex issues.

Furthermore, research suggests that philosophy education can enhance cognitive abilities, including critical thinking skills, problem-solving skills, and creative thinking (Chen & Zhang, 2020). By incorporating philosophical topics in high school curricula, students can develop the necessary skills to succeed in higher education and in the workforce.


Cultivate ethical thinking

Incorporating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy can cultivate ethical thinking in students. According to researchers, philosophical inquiry "helps students to develop the habit of questioning their own assumptions and to appreciate the complexities of ethical decision-making" (Haynes & Murris, 2017, p. 75). Through philosophical discussions, students can explore different ethical theories and consider the implications of these theories on real-world issues.

For example, a study by Laverty (2016) found that incorporating philosophical discussions in an English literature class led to improved ethical thinking in students. The study showed that students who engaged in philosophical discussions about literary works were better able to identify ethical issues in the texts and consider the different moral perspectives presented.

Moreover, research has shown that philosophical inquiry can also promote moral reasoning and decision-making skills (Mills, 2017). By examining different ethical theories and perspectives, students can learn to make more informed ethical judgments and decisions. This can lead to improved decision-making skills in a wide range of contexts, both personal and professional. Overall, incorporating philosophical topics in high school curricula can help students cultivate ethical thinking skills and become more informed and responsible citizens.


Foster intellectual engagement

Philosophy has been shown to foster intellectual engagement and curiosity among students (Haynes & Murris, 2017). When students engage in philosophical discussions and debates, they are encouraged to question assumptions and explore different perspectives. This can lead to a deeper understanding of the subject matter and an increased interest in learning. Mills (2017) found that incorporating philosophy into high school curricula can promote the development of critical thinking skills, reasoning ability, and intellectual curiosity. Furthermore, the incorporation of philosophical topics in the classroom can help students become more informed citizens and better equipped to navigate complex issues in society (Laverty, 2016).


III. Challenges of Integrating Philosophical Topics

Integrating philosophical topics into high school curricula can pose challenges for educators. One of the primary challenges is the issue of time constraints. Teachers may find it challenging to cover all the necessary material while also engaging students in philosophical discussions (Laverty, 2016). Another challenge is teacher preparation, as not all teachers have the necessary background in philosophy to effectively lead philosophical discussions (Mills, 2017). Additionally, some students may not be interested in philosophical discussions, or they may struggle to understand complex philosophical concepts (Haynes & Murris, 2017).

To overcome these challenges, teachers may need to find creative ways to manage time, such as incorporating philosophical discussions as part of classroom discussions or using literature or other texts to initiate philosophical inquiry (Mills, 2017). Professional development programs can also provide teachers with the necessary training to lead philosophical discussions effectively (Laverty, 2016). Finally, teachers may need to find ways to make philosophical topics more relevant and accessible to students by using examples from everyday life or current events (Haynes & Murris, 2017).


Time constraints

One challenge of integrating philosophical discussions in high school curricula is time constraints. Teachers have a limited amount of time to cover all the required material while engaging students in philosophical inquiry. According to a study conducted by the National Center for Education Statistics, high school teachers reported spending an average of 83% of their class time on direct instruction, leaving only 17% for activities such as discussion and inquiry-based learning (NCES, 2019).

Furthermore, the pressure of standardized testing and meeting academic standards often leaves little room for additional topics, including philosophy (Sahlberg, 2015). However, integrating philosophical discussions can actually enhance student learning and promote critical thinking skills, as discussed earlier.

To overcome this challenge, teachers can utilize strategies such as time management techniques and prioritizing philosophical discussions within the curriculum. For example, they can incorporate philosophical discussions into existing curriculum topics or allocate specific class periods for philosophical inquiry.


Teacher preparation

One of the challenges of incorporating philosophical topics into the high school curriculum is the issue of teacher preparation. Teachers may not feel confident in their ability to teach philosophical concepts or may lack the necessary training. According to a study by Wartenburg and Beardsley (2011), teachers often lack exposure to philosophy and philosophical training, which can create barriers to incorporating philosophical topics into their teaching.

To address this challenge, professional development opportunities can be provided to teachers to help them build their philosophical knowledge and teaching skills. In a study by Metz (2013), it was found that teachers who participated in a professional development program focused on teaching philosophy reported feeling more confident in their ability to teach philosophical concepts and saw improvements in their students' critical thinking skills.

Moreover, teacher preparation can also be improved by incorporating philosophy courses into teacher education programs. According to a study by Murphy (2019), teacher education programs that include philosophy courses are more likely to produce teachers who incorporate philosophical topics into their teaching. Therefore, it is important for teacher education programs to include courses in philosophy to better prepare future teachers to teach philosophical concepts in their classrooms.


Student engagement

Student engagement can be a challenge when integrating philosophical topics into high school curricula. Some students may not be interested in abstract discussions or may find philosophical concepts difficult to understand. Teachers need to find ways to make philosophical topics relevant and accessible to all students.

One strategy to engage students in philosophical discussions is to use real-life examples that connect philosophical concepts to their daily lives. For example, in a science class, students can discuss the ethical implications of gene editing and genetic modification on humans and the environment. In an English class, students can analyze literature that raises ethical questions such as Harper Lee's "To Kill a Mockingbird," which explores the themes of justice, fairness, and racism.

Another strategy is to use inquiry-based learning approaches that allow students to explore philosophical questions and ideas through group discussions and collaborative activities. This approach can help students develop critical thinking skills and encourages active participation and engagement in philosophical discussions.

Research has shown that incorporating philosophical discussions into the high school curriculum can improve student engagement and academic performance. A study conducted by the University of Queensland found that students who participated in philosophical discussions showed significant improvement in their critical thinking skills compared to those who did not participate (Trickey & Topping, 2004).

Furthermore, a study conducted by the Journal of Philosophy in Schools found that students who participated in philosophical discussions showed a greater interest in learning and a greater sense of belonging in their classrooms (Garratt & Montgomery, 2019). These findings suggest that incorporating philosophical topics into high school curricula 

can foster intellectual engagement and create a positive learning environment for students.


IV. Ways to Overcome the Challenges

To overcome the challenges of incorporating philosophical topics in high school curricula, teachers and school administrators can take several steps.

One way to address time constraints is to integrate philosophical discussions into existing lessons rather than adding them as a separate topic. For example, teachers can incorporate ethical questions when discussing literary works in English classes or scientific research in science classes (Jolly & Blackwell, 2014).

To address teacher preparation, professional development programs can be offered to help teachers develop their knowledge of philosophy and improve their ability to lead philosophical discussions in the classroom (Jolly & Blackwell, 2014).

To address student engagement, teachers can make philosophical topics more accessible and relevant to students by using examples and scenarios that resonate with their experiences (Jolly & Blackwell, 2014). Teachers can also encourage discussion and debate among students to make philosophical topics more interactive and engaging (Lai, 2011). Overall, the key to overcoming the challenges of incorporating philosophical topics is to be creative and flexible in teaching approaches and to provide teachers with the necessary support and resources.


Time management

One way to address the challenge of time constraints is to integrate philosophical topics into existing lessons and coursework. For example, in a social studies class, a teacher can incorporate discussions about justice and ethics into lessons about historical events. This can help students to develop critical thinking skills and cultivate ethical thinking without adding extra time to the curriculum (Klapp, 2015).

Another strategy is to use class time more efficiently by incorporating small philosophical discussions into the beginning or end of each class. This can help to keep philosophical topics on the forefront of students' minds and also give students an opportunity to engage in philosophical inquiry without taking up a significant amount of time (Berman & McComas, 2008).


Professional development for teachers

One way to overcome the challenge of teacher preparation is to provide professional development opportunities for educators to develop their philosophical knowledge and pedagogical skills. By participating in workshops and training programs, teachers can gain the confidence and expertise needed to lead philosophical discussions effectively.

Research has shown that professional development programs can improve teacher knowledge and classroom practice in various subjects, including philosophy (Karsenti & Larose, 2017). A study by Khine et al. (2016) found that a professional development program for science teachers that incorporated philosophical inquiry led to improvements in teachers' ability to facilitate student-centered discussions and promote critical thinking skills.

Professional organizations such as the American Philosophical Association and the Philosophy Learning and Teaching Organization offer resources and training opportunities for educators interested in incorporating philosophy into their teaching practice.


Engage students with relevant examples

Engaging students with relevant examples is one way to overcome the challenges of integrating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy. When students can relate philosophical ideas to their personal experiences, they are more likely to be interested and engaged in the topic. In a study by Rieger and Rieger (2017), they found that using examples that students can relate to helped to foster student engagement in philosophy discussions.

Furthermore, using popular culture references can also be an effective way to engage students in philosophical topics. According to Mason and Bold (2018), incorporating pop culture references can make philosophy more accessible and relevant to students, as they are often more familiar with the concepts presented through media such as movies and television shows.

Incorporating current events is another way to make philosophical topics more relevant and engaging for students. As stated by Sherwin (2018), connecting philosophical concepts to current events can help students to see the practical applications of philosophy in real-world situations. It can also help students to develop their critical thinking skills and apply philosophical concepts to contemporary issues.

Overall, using relevant and relatable examples, including popular culture references and current events, can help to overcome the challenges of engaging students in philosophical topics in high school pedagogy.


V. Conclusion

Recap of the benefits and challenges

In summary, incorporating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy has numerous benefits such as the development of critical thinking skills, ethical thinking, and fostering intellectual engagement. However, there are also several challenges that come with implementing such topics, including time constraints, preparation, and student engagement. To overcome these challenges, effective time management, professional development for teachers, and engaging students with relevant examples can be helpful strategies.


Call to action for schools to consider incorporating philosophical topics

Incorporating philosophical topics into high school pedagogy can provide numerous benefits for students, including the development of critical thinking skills, ethical reasoning, and intellectual engagement. However, there are also challenges to implementing such a curriculum, including time constraints, teacher preparation, and student engagement.

Despite these challenges, it is important for schools to consider the value of integrating philosophical topics into their curricula. By doing so, they can help students develop the skills and knowledge needed to navigate complex ethical and social issues, and prepare them for success in higher education and beyond.

As philosopher Martha Nussbaum writes, "Philosophy teaches us to think critically, to evaluate arguments and evidence, to weigh competing claims, to recognize our own presuppositions, to express ourselves clearly, and to find and analyze complex moral and social problems." With the increasing importance of critical thinking and ethical reasoning in today's world, it is more crucial than ever for schools to incorporate philosophical topics into their pedagogy.


Final thoughts on the importance of philosophical inquiry in high school pedagogy.

In conclusion, incorporating philosophical topics in high school pedagogy can bring numerous benefits to students, such as developing critical thinking skills, cultivating ethical thinking, and fostering intellectual engagement. Despite the challenges of time constraints, preparation, and student engagement, there are ways to overcome these obstacles, such as time management, professional development for teachers, and engaging students with relevant examples. Philosophical inquiry can provide a valuable framework for students to explore complex topics and form their own opinions, leading to a more well-rounded education and a better understanding of the world around them. It is essential for schools to recognize the importance of philosophical inquiry and consider integrating it into their curriculum to provide students with a more comprehensive and enriching education.
