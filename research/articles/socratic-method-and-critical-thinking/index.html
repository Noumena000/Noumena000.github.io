The Socratic Method and Critical Thinking

Benjamin A. Moss · Originally published on LinkedIn

View original on LinkedIn

The Socratic Method is an inquiry-based approach to teaching and learning that has been named after the Greek philosopher Socrates. This method involves questioning assumptions, examining evidence, and exploring alternative viewpoints, with the ultimate goal of promoting critical thinking and problem-solving skills. Socrates himself is renowned for his contribution to Western philosophy, specifically for his method of questioning, which he believed was necessary to arrive at a deeper truth. Although he did not leave any written works behind, much of his philosophy has been passed down through his students, particularly Plato.


The Socratic Method is a powerful tool for critical thinking as it encourages individuals to consider the reasons behind their beliefs and to evaluate whether those reasons are well-supported. This type of inquiry fosters intellectual humility and a willingness to admit ignorance, leading to greater learning and understanding. Additionally, it promotes evidence-based decision-making and consideration of alternative viewpoints. Therefore, the Socratic Method is an important tool for anyone seeking to develop their critical thinking skills and engage in meaningful discourse.


II. The Socratic Method


The pursuit of knowledge and understanding of the world and human existence was a central focus of Ancient Greek philosophy. One of the most influential philosophers of this time was Socrates, who developed a method of inquiry known as Elenchus, or the Socratic Method.


The Socratic Method is a type of questioning that seeks to uncover the underlying assumptions, beliefs, and values of an individual or group. Through a dialogue between two or more people, one person asks questions to help the other person clarify their thoughts and ideas.


Socrates used the Socratic Method to challenge people's beliefs and encourage critical thinking about their assumptions and the assumptions of others. He believed that true knowledge comes from questioning and examining one's own beliefs and that only through this process can a deeper understanding of the world be reached.


Today, the Socratic Method is still used as a tool for critical thinking, problem-solving, and self-reflection. It allows individuals to examine their own assumptions and biases, as well as those of others, to arrive at a more accurate and well-rounded understanding of a particular issue or concept.


Unlike debate, which is often adversarial and seeks to prove one side right and the other wrong, the Socratic Method is collaborative and aims to help the interlocutor arrive at a better understanding of a topic. The focus is on questioning assumptions and exploring the implications of the interlocutor's beliefs, rather than making statements and providing arguments to support them.


Through the Socratic Method, Socrates aimed to arrive at a clearer understanding of concepts and to expose the limitations of existing knowledge. This involved defining key terms and concepts, as well as refuting claims that are unsupported by evidence or logical reasoning.


In the Socratic Method, the interlocutor is the person with whom Socrates engages in a dialogue. Socrates uses questioning to help the interlocutor examine their beliefs and assumptions, often leading to a state of confusion or doubt known as aporia. This state of confusion is seen as necessary to break down preconceptions and biases, and to create a space for new insights and perspectives to emerge.


Socrates believed that true knowledge is recognizing one's own ignorance, and that wisdom comes from questioning and examining one's own beliefs. To encourage his interlocutors to examine their own beliefs and assumptions more closely, Socrates sometimes used a technique called Socratic irony, in which he pretended to be ignorant or uncertain about a topic. This allowed him to expose inconsistencies in his interlocutor's arguments and push them towards a deeper understanding of the topic at hand.


III. Applications of the Socratic Method


Socratic irony is a powerful teaching technique employed by Socrates to prompt critical thinking among his students. Through this technique, Socrates pretended to be ignorant or unsure about a subject to encourage his interlocutors to think more deeply and critically about their own beliefs and assumptions. By exposing the flaws in their reasoning, Socrates helped them to recognize their own limitations in understanding complex ideas, creating intellectual humility that made them more receptive to new ideas and ways of thinking.


Today, Socratic irony is still used in teaching methods that emphasize critical thinking, particularly in philosophy. This technique encourages students to question their beliefs and assumptions, to be open to new perspectives, and to think critically about the evidence and arguments presented to them. 


To illustrate this, imagine a group of students discussing the ethics of animal testing. A Socratic teacher might engage them in a dialogue using the Socratic Method, asking questions to challenge their assumptions and promote critical thinking. This dialogue might lead the students to question their own beliefs and consider alternative perspectives, leading to a deeper understanding of the issue at hand.


In the Socratic Method, a claim is a statement or proposition that the speaker asserts as true, while a counterclaim is a statement or proposition that contradicts the original claim. Through questioning and challenging the counterclaim, Socrates seeks to arrive at a revised claim that has been refined or improved through critical inquiry. The ultimate goal is not to arrive at a final answer, but rather to engage in a continuous process of questioning and seeking understanding.


IV. The Socratic Method is an essential component of critical thinking and studying it can yield numerous benefits. 


Individuals can develop a deeper understanding of complex issues by continually questioning and challenging assumptions. Through this process, hidden biases can be uncovered, leading to a more nuanced understanding of complex ideas. Additionally, the Socratic Method can help individuals to construct more effective counterarguments and engage in rational discussions. It emphasizes the importance of examining evidence and reasoning logically, which can lead to more productive problem-solving.


Studying the Socratic Method also encourages individuals to reflect upon their own presuppositions and biases. By challenging their beliefs and considering alternative perspectives, individuals can become more open-minded and humble in their pursuit of knowledge. This can help them to identify any inconsistencies or contradictions in their beliefs and work to resolve them, leading to a more coherent and rational worldview.


Moreover, the Socratic Method is crucial in creating consistency in one's beliefs, which is fundamental to the pursuit of knowledge. By examining their beliefs and questioning their coherence, individuals can create a more accurate understanding of the world. This leads to greater knowledge and promotes critical thinking, which can be beneficial in decision-making and problem-solving.


In conclusion, studying the Socratic Method is important for individuals who want to become more effective critical thinkers and problem-solvers. By questioning assumptions, reflecting upon biases, and constructing effective counterarguments, individuals can gain a deeper understanding of complex issues and develop more coherent beliefs. Through the pursuit of knowledge and critical thinking, individuals can continue to challenge and refine their ideas, which can lead to growth and greater intellectual humility.


V. Conclusion 


In conclusion, the Socratic Method is an indispensable tool for critical thinking, as it involves questioning presuppositions, refining definitions, and refuting claims. Through the use of aporia, paradox of knowledge, and Socratic irony, the method enables individuals to engage in rational discourse and self-reflection, which are essential for intellectual growth. 


Socrates' legacy in philosophy remains influential today, and his emphasis on knowledge and humility underscores the importance of critical thinking. By studying the Socratic Method, individuals can develop their critical thinking skills and cultivate a deeper understanding of complex issues. 


Overall, the Socratic Method provides a robust framework for analyzing our beliefs and assumptions in a systematic and rational manner. By engaging in this method, individuals can create a more consistent and coherent worldview, and continue to learn and grow intellectually.
