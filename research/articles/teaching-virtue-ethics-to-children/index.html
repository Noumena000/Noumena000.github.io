Teaching Virtue Ethics to Children: The Moral Cultivation of Virtues

Benjamin A. Moss · Originally published on LinkedIn

View original on LinkedIn

Teaching children about ethical behavior and moral character is an essential component of their development. Virtue ethics provides a framework for understanding the cultivation of moral character and the development of virtues, which are habits of character that enable individuals to act in accordance with ethical principles. The four cardinal virtues of prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance are essential components of the development of moral character, and are particularly relevant to the upbringing of children. By teaching children how to cultivate these virtues, parents and educators can instill in them habits of ethical behavior and moral character that will serve them throughout their lives. This essay will focus on the moral cultivation of the cardinal virtues of prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance, and will explore ways in which these virtues can be taught to children. Specifically, we will examine how parents and educators can teach children to cultivate these virtues by utilizing reason and moral guidance, as well as by providing them with opportunities to practice virtuous behavior.


What are Virtues? 

The cultivation of virtues involves developing a set of habits that are conducive to living a virtuous life. Virtues are habits of behavior that enable individuals to act in ways that are morally good and desirable. They are qualities of character that help people to achieve excellence in various areas of life, including moral and intellectual domains. The four cardinal virtues are prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance (Aristotle, 350 BCE).

Prudence involves making wise decisions by using practical reason to determine the best course of action. It is the ability to see what is right and wrong, and to act accordingly. Teaching children the importance of prudence involves instilling in them the habit of careful deliberation and the ability to make informed decisions. This can be done by encouraging children to think critically about their actions and the consequences that may result from them (Hursthouse, 1991).

Justice is the virtue of giving what is due to others (Nussbaum, 1990). It involves treating people fairly and impartially. Teaching children the importance of justice involves instilling in them a sense of fairness and the ability to consider the needs and interests of others. This can be done by encouraging children to develop empathy and to think about how their actions may affect others.

Fortitude is the virtue of courage and perseverance in the face of adversity (Aristotle, 350 BCE). It involves the ability to overcome fear and to act bravely in difficult situations. Teaching children the importance of fortitude involves encouraging them to develop resilience and to face challenges with courage and determination.

Temperance is the virtue of self-control and moderation (Aristotle, 350 BCE). It involves the ability to regulate one's desires and impulses in order to achieve balance and harmony in life. Teaching children the importance of temperance involves instilling in them the habit of self-control and the ability to resist temptation.

Virtues can be taught with the right moral guidance by utilizing reason and focusing on the cardinal virtues of prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance. By instilling these virtues in children, they will be better equipped to make moral decisions and live virtuous lives.


How can Prudence be Taught? 

Prudence is an essential virtue in the development of moral character, as it enables individuals to make wise decisions based on practical reasoning abilities (Aristotle, 350 BCE). Teaching children how to cultivate the virtue of prudence involves instilling in them the habit of careful deliberation and the ability to make informed decisions based on their moral values and principles (Hursthouse, 1991).

One effective way to teach children how to cultivate the virtue of prudence is by engaging them in discussions that help them consider the ethical implications of their actions and guide them to make decisions that align with their moral values (Nussbaum, 1986). Parents and educators can encourage children to think critically about the potential outcomes of their decisions and consider how their actions might affect others (McIntyre, 1984). This helps children develop the ability to make informed decisions based on practical reasoning abilities.

Another way to teach children how to cultivate the virtue of prudence is by modeling prudent behavior in our own actions. Children learn by example, so it is essential to model prudent behavior by making wise decisions, considering the consequences of our actions, and acting in accordance with our moral principles (Curren, 2010). By observing adults making prudent decisions, children can learn to adopt similar habits of careful deliberation and decision-making.

Furthermore, parents and educators can teach children about the importance of prudence by providing them with opportunities to practice decision-making skills in safe and supportive environments. By gradually increasing their responsibility for decision-making, children can develop the habit of careful deliberation and gain confidence in their ability to make informed decisions (Nucci, 2001).

I believe that Teaching children about the importance of prudence can be achieved through engaging them in discussions about the ethical implications of their actions, modeling prudent behavior in our own actions, and providing them with opportunities to practice decision-making skills. These methods help children develop the habit of careful deliberation and the ability to make informed decisions based on practical reasoning abilities, which are essential components of the development of moral character.


How can Justice be Taught?

Justice is a cardinal virtue in virtue ethics that involves treating others fairly and giving them what they deserve (Aristotle, 350 BCE). It is essential to teach children the importance of justice, as it helps them develop a sense of empathy and respect for others, which are essential components of the development of moral character.

One effective way to teach children the importance of justice is by engaging them in discussions about fairness and equality. Parents and educators can help children understand that treating others fairly is essential for maintaining positive relationships and creating a just and harmonious society (Nussbaum, 1997). Children can be encouraged to think about how their actions affect others and how they can make a positive impact on their community by treating others with respect and fairness (Puka, 2010).

Another way to teach children the importance of justice is by modeling just behavior in our own actions. Children learn by example, so it is essential to model just behavior by treating others fairly and with respect, and standing up for what is right (Curren, 2010). When children see their parents and educators acting justly, they are more likely to develop a sense of moral responsibility and accountability, which are essential components of the development of moral character.

Additionally, children can be taught about the principles of justice through stories and literature that illustrate the importance of treating others fairly and standing up for what is right. By reading stories and discussing the characters’ actions and motivations, children can learn about the consequences of both just and unjust behavior (Nussbaum, 1997).

Teaching children about the importance of justice can be achieved through engaging them in discussions about fairness and equality, modeling just behavior in our own actions, and using literature to illustrate the principles of justice. These methods help children develop a sense of empathy, respect, and moral responsibility, which are essential components of the development of moral character.


How can Fortitude be Taught? 

Fortitude is a key virtue that enables individuals to overcome obstacles and difficulties, to persevere in the face of adversity, and to act courageously in pursuit of their goals (Aristotle, 350 BCE). Teaching children how to cultivate the virtue of fortitude involves instilling in them the habit of facing challenges with courage and resilience, and developing the determination and perseverance to pursue their goals despite setbacks (Lickona, 1991).

One effective way to teach children how to cultivate the virtue of fortitude is by providing them with opportunities to face challenges and difficulties that are appropriate to their age and development. Parents and educators can encourage children to take on new challenges and to persevere in the face of setbacks, providing support and guidance as needed (Berkowitz & Grych, 1998). Through these experiences, children can develop the courage and resilience to face challenges with confidence and determination.

Furthermore, parents and educators can teach children about the importance of fortitude by modeling courageous behavior in our own actions. Children learn by example, so it is essential to model fortitude by facing challenges with courage and resilience, and persevering in the face of adversity (Curren, 2010). By observing adults demonstrating fortitude, children can learn to adopt similar habits of courage and perseverance.

In addition, parents and educators can teach children about the importance of fortitude by providing them with moral stories and role models that exemplify courageous behavior. These stories and role models can inspire children to develop the courage and resilience to face challenges and difficulties in their own lives (Berkowitz & Bier, 2007).

I have argued that teaching children about the importance of fortitude can be achieved through providing them with opportunities to face challenges and difficulties, modeling courageous behavior in our own actions, and providing them with moral stories and role models that exemplify the virtue of fortitude. These methods help children develop the courage and resilience to face challenges with confidence and determination, which are essential components of the development of moral character.


How can Temperance be Taught?

Temperance is the virtue that enables individuals to exercise self-control, to moderate their desires and appetites, and to develop habits of restraint and moderation (Aristotle, 350 BCE). Teaching children how to cultivate the virtue of temperance involves instilling in them the habit of self-control and moderation, and developing the ability to resist temptation and delay gratification (Lickona, 1991).

One effective way to teach children how to cultivate the virtue of temperance is by modeling self-control and moderation in our own actions. Parents and educators can demonstrate the importance of temperance by exercising self-control and moderation in their own behavior, whether it be in terms of food, drink, or other indulgences (Curren, 2010). By modeling self-control and moderation, children can learn to adopt similar habits of restraint and moderation.

Another way to teach children about the importance of temperance is by providing them with opportunities to practice self-control and moderation in their own lives. Parents and educators can encourage children to delay gratification and to resist temptation in age-appropriate ways, such as waiting their turn, sharing with others, or saving money for a future goal (Berkowitz & Grych, 1998). Through these experiences, children can develop the ability to resist temptation and delay gratification, which are important components of the development of self-control and temperance.

Furthermore, parents and educators can teach children about the importance of temperance by discussing the consequences of excessive indulgence and lack of self-control. By explaining the negative effects that come from indulging in excess, such as poor health, diminished self-esteem, and harm to others, children can develop an understanding of why temperance is important (Curren, 2010).

In addition, parents and educators can teach children about the importance of temperance by providing them with moral stories and role models that exemplify the virtue of temperance. These stories and role models can inspire children to develop the ability to exercise self-control and moderation in their own lives (Berkowitz & Bier, 2007).

The importance of temperance can be achieved through modeling self-control and moderation, providing them with opportunities to practice self-control and moderation, discussing the consequences of excessive indulgence, and providing them with moral stories and role models that exemplify the virtue of temperance. These methods help children develop the ability to exercise self-control and moderation, which are essential components of the development of moral character.


Conclusion

In conclusion, teaching virtue ethics to children is essential for their development of moral character and ethical behavior. The cultivation of the cardinal virtues of prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance provides a framework for understanding the development of moral character and the habits of ethical behavior that enable individuals to act in accordance with ethical principles. By teaching children how to cultivate these virtues, parents and educators can instill in them the habits of ethical behavior and moral character that will serve them throughout their lives. The process of teaching these virtues to children involves utilizing reason and moral guidance, providing opportunities for them to practice virtuous behavior, and modeling virtuous behavior as parents and educators. By doing so, we can help to create a generation of individuals who are committed to living lives of ethical behavior and moral character. Ultimately, this will benefit both the individuals themselves and society as a whole.
