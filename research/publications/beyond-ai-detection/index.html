Abstract

Generative AI complicates academic-integrity decisions because writing alone rarely reveals how it was produced. This methodological paper examines philosophically contextualized instructions embedded in online applied-ethics prompts and proposes a controlled protocol to test copying, model response, accidental marker occurrence, and review-workflow behavior. The documented record describes one clipboard-transfer observation, which does not establish model compliance, detection accuracy, or unauthorized assistance. The paper argues that a marker match can prompt contextual human review but cannot independently establish authorship or misconduct.
